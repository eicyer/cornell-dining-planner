# Generate candidates from a benchmark protein/carb/vegetable plate model, not one item per dining-hall station

Crafted Meals were coming back either too varied (up to `MAX_CATEGORIES=5` items, one per dining-hall category) or with disproportionate portions (a bounded least-squares solve sizing a "side" anywhere from 40g to 250g purely to minimize macro error). Inspecting live menu data showed why: `MenuItem.category` is a dining-hall station name, not a food role, and stations routinely mix things that should never share a plate slot — Cornell's "Salad" category contains dressing, tofu, and rice; "Desserts" contains waffles, cereal, and bagels alongside actual dessert; "Grill" contains a scrambled-tofu entree next to caramel sauce. `generate_item_sets` ([[0003-meal-crafting-algorithm]]) picked the first five distinct categories a menu event happened to return rows in and drew one item from each, with no notion of whether those five stations formed a coherent plate — so a candidate could genuinely be protein + rice + a waffle-station cookie + a fruit-bar yogurt + a salad dressing sized like a side. `_solve_bounded`'s bounded least-squares (`scipy.optimize.lsq_linear`) then had no way to tell a 20g condiment from a 200g vegetable apart from macro fit, so a dressing or syrup could get sized to 150-250g chasing a carb target.

We considered keeping category-based grouping and just patching the two symptoms — excluding more categories from `NON_MEAL_CATEGORIES`, tightening `_solve_bounded`'s bounds, adding a regularization term to pull portions toward a mid-range prior. That would have reduced the damage but not the cause: station name still wouldn't tell you whether an item is a protein, a starch, or a garnish, so the next mismatched station would reproduce the same failure mode. Two nutrition/portion benchmarks pointed at a more direct fix instead: USDA MyPlate models a meal as vegetable + grain + protein in roughly comparable shares, and the hand-portion method (1-2 palms protein, 1-2 fists vegetable, 1-2 cupped hands carb) is the standard coaching heuristic for the same three-role split — both describe a plate by food role, not by where a dining hall happened to shelve it. Separately, sensory-specific-satiety research shows that intake rises with the sheer number of distinct foods on a plate regardless of macros (the "buffet effect") — so capping variety isn't just about looking coherent, it works against the calorie/macro target itself if left unbounded.

Decision: `app/services/meal_crafting.py::classify_role` buckets each item into `protein` / `carb` / `vegetable` / `None` from its own nutrition numbers and name, not its station:
- **Protein**: `protein_g_per_100g >= PROTEIN_DENSITY_MIN` (8.0), the same signal the old `_pick_anchor` used, restricted away from `salad`/`soup`/`chef's table - sides` (`PROTEIN_ROLE_EXCLUDED_CATEGORIES`) — carried over from the old `ANCHOR_EXCLUDED_CATEGORIES` because a mismatched USDA match on a salad-bar item is more likely to produce a bogus protein reading than an actual entree is (confirmed live: a broccoli side matched to 15g protein/100g would otherwise become the "protein" anchor).
- **Vegetable**: not excluded/protein, and `calories_per_100g < VEGETABLE_MAX_CALORIES_PER_100G` (80) — non-starchy vegetables run ~15-45 kcal/100g, starchy vegetables and cooked grains ~70-140+, so 80 separates the bands without a food-database dependency.
- **Carb**: everything else that isn't excluded — starches, grains, breads.
- **Excluded** (`None`): `EXCLUDED_ROLE_CATEGORIES` (beverages, coffee, condiments, desserts, fruit & yogurt bar, bagel bar/cereal — desserts/fruit-yogurt are new exclusions here, since without them a low-calorie-density dessert item could pass the vegetable check) and `CONDIMENT_KEYWORDS` — a name-substring list (`dressing`, `sauce`, `syrup`, `gravy`, ...) catching seasoning-scale items that slip past the category/density checks, same idiom as `WHOLE_FOODS_PENALIZED_KEYWORDS` in [[0009-deterministic-preference-preranking]]. Excluded items never enter candidate generation, the same mechanism `NON_MEAL_CATEGORIES` already used for beverages — nothing new architecturally, just a wider and role-driven net.

`generate_item_sets` now draws at most one item per available role (`ROLE_ORDER = protein, carb, vegetable`) instead of up to five per category — a role missing from an eatery's offerings is skipped, never backfilled with an unrelated substitute. `solve_portions` sizes each role directly off whatever's left of its own driving number after earlier roles are accounted for — protein off the remaining protein target (unchanged from the old anchor), carb off the remaining carb target, vegetable off the remaining *calorie* target (vegetables aren't chosen to hit a specific macro) — each clamped to a role-specific benchmark range (`ANCHOR_MIN/MAX_GRAMS` 100-280 unchanged; new `CARB_MIN/MAX_GRAMS` 100-250; new `VEG_MIN/MAX_GRAMS` 80-220, both narrower bands than the old blanket 40-250 `SIDE` range so a single item can't swing from garnish to mountain). This is the "adjust grams by calorie density" behavior: dividing a remaining macro/calorie budget by the specific item's density-per-100g is exactly what already existed for the protein anchor, just generalized to the other two roles instead of being replaced by a joint least-squares solve. Because every candidate now has at most one item per role, this direct division replaces `_solve_bounded`'s `lsq_linear` call entirely — `scipy`/`numpy` are no longer a dependency of this module (or the project — nothing else imported them).

None of this touches Hard Constraint filtering (`passes_hard_constraints`/`filter_items`, [[0003-meal-crafting-algorithm]], run first and unchanged) or Soft Preference ranking (`app/services/preference_scoring.py` scores whatever `MealCandidate`s come out of this, unaware of how they were assembled, per [[0009-deterministic-preference-preranking]]) — `generate_candidates`'s signature and the `MealCandidate`/`CraftedItem` shape are unchanged, so neither of those, nor `meal_polish.py`, needed any changes.

## Addendum: bound protein/carb sizing by the remaining calorie budget, not just the macro target

Sizing the protein anchor purely off the remaining protein target (unchanged from the original [[0003-meal-crafting-algorithm]] anchor) meant a calorie-dense protein source chasing a still-unmet protein target could blow well past the meal's calorie target while landing right on protein — confirmed live: a 325 kcal/100g breakfast sausage sized toward a 45g protein target came out to 1535 total candidate calories against a 700 target.

We considered switching the anchor to a joint calories+protein least-squares solve (closer to the old `_solve_bounded`), but that reintroduces the exact numerical-solver opacity this ADR moved away from, for a two-variable case that doesn't need it. Decision: `_grams_for_remaining` takes an optional `calorie_bound` — `(remaining_calories, calories_per_100g)` for that same item — and clamps grams so the item can't use more of the meal's calorie budget than that bound allows, even if the macro target would ask for more. Meeting the macro target is still the goal (the calorie bound only binds when the two disagree), so a lean, low-calorie-density protein source is unaffected. Both protein and carb reserve the *other* still-unfilled role's floor-gram calories before computing their own bound (`reserved_calories` / `veg_reserved_calories` in `solve_portions`) — otherwise the anchor could still legally spend the entire remaining budget and leave carb/veg's own minimum realistic portions (`CARB_MIN_GRAMS`/`VEG_MIN_GRAMS`) to overshoot on top of it. Vegetable is sized last and already solves directly against remaining calories, so it needs no separate bound.

Re-running the same live-menu case: 1535 → 700 total calories against the 700 target, with protein landing at 80g (over-target, since two role items both happened to carry real protein) instead of the prior calorie blowout. The tradeoff is explicit and intentional: when an eatery's only protein-role item is very calorie-dense, the meal now protects the calorie target and may undershoot the protein target rather than the reverse — consistent with Health Goal ([[CONTEXT.md]]) being expressed primarily as a calorie target, with macros as the secondary split of it.

## Known limitation: still garbage-in-garbage-out on bad nutrition matches

Role classification is only as good as the underlying `NutritionMatch` numbers ([[0001-nutrition-data-source]]/[[0002-enrichment-pipeline]]); a badly-matched item can still land in the wrong role bucket (e.g. a mismatched rice entry reading low-protein-low-calorie could misclassify as `vegetable`). `PROTEIN_ROLE_EXCLUDED_CATEGORIES` blocks the worst case (a bad match becoming the protein anchor) but doesn't fully immunize the other two roles — pre-existing from [[0003-meal-crafting-algorithm]], not introduced here.
